Urban Oasis: cooling Manchester's streets

A dissertation that tests heat-mitigation toolkits on narrow city-centre streets with thermal-comfort simulation, then turns the results into a masterplan and design guide.

Location
Manchester city centre, UK
Area
10 ha
Context
MSc dissertation, The University of Manchester, 2023
Methods
Automated UTCI simulation in Python, Rhino and Grasshopper

Highlights

  • An automated UTCI simulation workflow in Python, Rhino and Grasshopper
  • Modular sidewalk prototypes combining shade, greenery and water
  • A prioritisation model for where shade goes first

Replacing vegetation with hard surfaces makes cities several degrees warmer than the countryside around them. Manchester, shaped by industry and short on green space, feels this sharply in summer. This dissertation builds a repeatable workflow for retrofitting narrow streets to keep people comfortable.

Analysis

I built an automated Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) simulation workflow in Python, Rhino and Grasshopper and used it to find hotspots across the city centre. Studies of urban fabric and traffic around the Arndale Centre, Market Street and Shudehill set the context, and eight design principles followed: bioclimatic design, urban geometry, thermal comfort, walkability, sustainability, cultural context, activity and safety.

Testing

Combinations of shading, green infrastructure and blue infrastructure were simulated as modular sidewalk prototypes, then tested in three implementation scenarios based on current street policy. The best fit for each street became the final masterplan, a decision-making toolkit and a design guide for Manchester's varied streets.
