Urban Oasis: cooling Manchester's streets

A dissertation that tests heat-mitigation toolkits on narrow city-centre streets with thermal-comfort simulation, then turns the results into a masterplan and design guide.

Location
Manchester city centre, UK
Area
10 ha
Context
MSc dissertation, The University of Manchester
Methods
UTCI thermal comfort simulation, scenario testing

Highlights

  • UTCI simulation to locate heat hotspots
  • Modular sidewalk prototypes combining shade, greenery and water
  • A prioritisation model for where shade goes first

Replacing vegetation with hard surfaces makes cities several degrees warmer than the countryside around them. Manchester, shaped by industry and short on green space, feels this sharply in summer. This dissertation builds a repeatable workflow for retrofitting narrow streets to keep people comfortable.

Analysis

Universal Thermal Climate Index simulations identified hotspots across the city centre. Studies of urban fabric and traffic around the Arndale Centre, Market Street and Shudehill set the context, and eight design principles followed: bioclimatic design, urban geometry, thermal comfort, walkability, sustainability, cultural context, activity and safety.

Testing

Combinations of shading, green infrastructure and blue infrastructure were simulated as modular sidewalk prototypes, then tested in three implementation scenarios based on current street policy. The best fit for each street became the final masterplan and a design guide for Manchester's varied streets.
