Problem

Creators usually choose topics by instinct, which fails in crowded markets. Raw view counts don't help either: a big channel gets views because it is big, not because the topic has demand.

The question was how to measure demand independently of channel size, and how to flag markets that look busy but are held by a few giant channels.

Approach

  1. 01

    Parallel ingestion

    For a topic and region the app finds the most relevant channels, then fetches each channel's uploads and statistics concurrently. API calls are I/O-bound, so a thread pool cuts wall-clock time roughly in proportion to the number of channels.

  2. 02

    Outliers against each channel's own baseline

    A video counts as an outlier when it beats its own channel's median by a chosen multiple. Its viral multiplier, views divided by that median, measures demand without rewarding subscriber count. News-style uploads from million-subscriber channels with few views are filtered out, and each channel contributes at most two videos so one creator can't dominate the sample.

  3. 03

    A score that needs both volume and intensity

    Depth rewards the number of outliers, intensity rewards how far they beat their baselines, and a 30% penalty applies when channels classed as sharks make up more than half of the outliers.

Results

The Streamlit app turns this into four views: a single-topic dashboard with the score gauge, an opportunity map of upload date against viral multiplier, a tag explorer for long-tail keywords, and a head-to-head comparison of two topics.
