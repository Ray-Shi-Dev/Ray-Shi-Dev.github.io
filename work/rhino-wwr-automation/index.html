Problem

Window-to-wall ratio is a basic input to every building energy model and to LEED and EEWH submissions. Measuring it by hand from a 3D model is slow and error-prone, because architectural models are built to look right, not to be measured.

Three things break naive scripts: slabs are full of holes for cores and shafts, adjacent thermal zones leave gaps of 1 to 10 mm that make Boolean unions fail, and atriums and curtain walls span several floors, so their glazing belongs to more than one storey.

Approach

  1. 01

    Find the real floors

    The script scans layer names to separate thermal zones, underground zones and the glazing layer. Zones are grouped by base elevation with a 1.5 m tolerance; anything below ground or under 2 m tall is treated as a modelling artefact. Floor height comes from the gap to the next floor, so mezzanines and double-height levels come out right.

  2. 02

    Measure the facade, not the holes

    Each floor is sliced 1.2 m above its slab. The section curves are unioned with a 0.1 m tolerance to close micro-gaps, and only the largest closed ring is kept as the facade perimeter, which discards cores and shafts. If the union still fails, it falls back to the bounding-box perimeter instead of crashing.

  3. 03

    Split tall glazing by overlap

    A window spanning several storeys is divided by how much of its height overlaps each floor, so a three-storey curtain wall adds a third of its area to each level.

Results

Redraw is switched off while it runs, and the result is written as one CSV row per floor: elevation, height, gross perimeter, wall area, glazing area and WWR. The sample tower below ranges from 39.4% on the ground floor to 65.3% on floor 6.

My role

Solo project, written for my own energy-modelling work: geometry logic, edge-case handling and the CSV export.
