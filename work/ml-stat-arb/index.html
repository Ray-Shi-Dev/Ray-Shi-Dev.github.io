Machine-learning pairs trading for PropTech

A research prototype that trains a gradient-boosting ensemble to predict when a REIT pair's cointegration is about to break, and uses it to veto the pairs trades that would lose.

Problem

Classic pairs trading assumes the spread between two cointegrated assets reverts to its mean, so it enters when the spread's z-score passes two standard deviations. The strategy loses most when that assumption fails and the relationship breaks down.

This project asks whether the gradient-boosting pipeline I studied for building-energy anomaly detection can predict those breakdowns, and veto the trades they would wreck.

Approach

  1. 01

    Find candidate pairs

    Daily prices for liquid US REITs from 2015 to 2023 come from Yahoo Finance. K-means on standardised returns, with the cluster count chosen by the elbow method, groups similar REITs. Within each cluster an OLS hedge ratio on log prices and an augmented Dickey-Fuller test keep the pairs whose spread is stationary.

  2. 02

    Features without look-ahead

    The spread gets 21-day rolling statistics, lag differences and ratios, and a Savitzky-Golay smoothing that is made causal by applying the filter over a rolling window and keeping only its last point, so no feature sees the future. The target marks a breakdown when the spread five days ahead sits more than three rolling standard deviations from its mean.

  3. 03

    Ensemble as a veto, not a signal

    XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost are trained on the first 70% of the history, with the majority class downsampled, and their probabilities are averaged on the remaining 30%. The baseline proposes trades at |z| > 2; the overlay vetoes any trade whose predicted breakdown probability is 0.70 or higher.

Results

The pipeline reports out-of-sample ROC-AUC and how many baseline trades the overlay vetoes. It is a research prototype: results are not yet published in the repository.

My role

Solo research prototype: pair selection, feature design, model ensemble and the veto logic.
