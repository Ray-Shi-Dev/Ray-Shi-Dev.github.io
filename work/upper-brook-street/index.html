Work/Earlier work: urban design

A new quarter for Upper Brook Street

A 3.2-hectare masterplan that replaces vacant buildings with a walkable mixed-use neighbourhood between Manchester's campuses and city centre.

Location
Manchester, UK
Area
3.2 ha
Context
MSc studio, The University of Manchester
Programme
About 1,320 homes, offices, retail, community centre

Highlights

  • Pedestrian priority and a connected public-space network
  • Two phases at 200 and 110 dwellings per hectare
  • Green infrastructure and drainage that also buffer noise

The site sits between Manchester's north and south campuses and the city centre, yet abandoned buildings and traffic-heavy roads cut it off. The masterplan replaces them with a liveable quarter built around pedestrian priority, sustainable transport and well-connected public space.

Process

Strategic and area analysis covered policy, socio-economic context, routes, uses, green and blue infrastructure, and heritage. A SWOT and an opportunities-and-constraints map led to eight design objectives, which were weighed against each other through design options before the final plan was chosen.

Proposal

Perimeter blocks deliver around 1,320 homes across two phases, with 26,300 m² of offices, ground-floor retail and a community centre. New green infrastructure fills gaps in the existing network, Gartside Garden becomes a shared community space, and a clear street hierarchy with passive surveillance keeps the public realm safe.
