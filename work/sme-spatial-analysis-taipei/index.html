Where small businesses cluster in Taipei

Spatial autocorrelation, regression and bivariate LISA show that banks and commercial land, not transit, decide where SMEs locate.

Location
Taipei, Taiwan
Area
27,000 ha
Unit of analysis
Village (li)
Methods
Bivariate Moran's I, multiple linear regression, bivariate LISA

Highlights

  • Banks and commercial land significant at p < 0.05
  • Transit and population density showed spatially random links and were dropped
  • Hotspots in Neihu Technology Park and the Taipei Station hub

Small and medium-sized enterprises drive much of Taiwan's income and employment, and the Ministry of Economic Affairs ranks business-district location as the factor that has mattered most to their success over the past decade. This study asks which conditions draw them to particular parts of Taipei.

Method

Using villages as the unit, I tested company density against population density, street intersections, MRT stations, bus stops, banks and commercial land. Bivariate Moran's I with permutation tests screened out variables whose relationship was spatially random. A multiple linear regression tested what remained, and bivariate LISA mapped where each relationship clusters.

Findings

Banks and commercial land were the significant predictors. The filtered model had a higher adjusted R² than the full one. High-high hotspots sit in Neihu Technology Park, near Songshan Airport, in the Nanjing–Fuxing financial district and around Taipei Station; cold spots follow the hills and reserves of Beitou, Shilin, Nangang and Wenshan. Economic factors outweigh the built environment, which points SME policy toward banking access and commercial zoning.
